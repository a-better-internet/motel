# The Attraction — a second outline

The alternative to `PLOT.md`. Same brief — a game that *works* like Myst: one
person, no dialogue, no combat, a world you read — but a different story and,
more importantly, a different **verb**.

`PLOT.md` is a chain. Each place hands you the next place, you end up at an
order you have to enter, and the world is basically true: there was a bargain,
it worked, here are the consequences.

This one is a **case**. The world is lying to you on purpose, the evidence is
over-supplied rather than gated, and the thing you are actually doing is
deciding what is real. There is nothing supernatural in it anywhere, which is
what makes the last twenty minutes bad.

---

## 1 · The premise

Route 66 ran on manufactured wonder. Every mile of it had a sign for a cavern,
a ghost town, a snake farm, a petrified forest, a trading post — and most of
those were somebody's barn with a tin dinosaur out front. **The world already
has ten of those brochures printed in it** (`pic:brochure:0`–`9`: CAVERNS
TOUR, TRADING POST, GHOST TOWN, INDIAN CITY, LAKE CRUISES, GUN MUSEUM, SNAKE
FARM, MERAMEC CAVERNS, PETRIFIED FOREST, JACKRABBIT) and every one of them says
OPEN DAILY.

In 1972 the interstate opened forty miles north and took the traffic. By 1976
the drive-in was shut, the filling station was down to one working pump and the
motel was turning four rooms a week.

So they made an eleventh brochure.

Six people — the motel, the bar, the station, the drive-in, the man who painted
the signs, and somebody's brother-in-law with a camera — put together an
attraction. They picked the old silver adit in the hills west of the burying
ground because it was atmospheric and because nobody owned it. Over eight
months in 1977 they built a chamber at the end of it: an altar, a ring of nine
stakes, a circle and a star in ochre, an eye on the far wall, charms on cords,
and a hundred and twenty tally marks cut into the rock by the door. They aged
all of it. They shot a short film to run at the drive-in as a lure. They dressed
nine rooms at the motel so the tour could walk through them and see where "they"
had stayed. They printed the brochure and they put it in every rack for two
hundred miles.

It worked for one season and then it stopped, suddenly, in August 1979, and
nobody ever took the signs down.

**You are not a tourist.** The land is going to auction and somebody has to walk
the parcel and write down what is on it. You have a clipboard, a list of
twenty-four rooms, and no opinion.

**Tone:** a procedural. Dry, unhurried, faintly comic in the first hour —
finding the receipts for a haunting is funny — and then it stops being funny
and does not start again.

## 2 · The verb

`PLOT.md`'s verb is *remember*: you carry knowledge forward and eventually type
it in. This one's verb is **date it**.

Everything in this world can be dated by somebody who knows how to look, and
learning how to look is the actual game:

| Tell | Where you learn it |
|---|---|
| A price printed on a thing | the snack bar's unsold stock |
| The seam on a bottle | the crate of empties behind the Canteen |
| A model year | the cars in the lot |
| A meter's serial and its last read | the filling station |
| Newspaper used as drawer liner | any motel room |
| Paint under paint, where a sign was redone | the pole sign, the station |
| Whether dust has a shadow behind an object | the nine dressed rooms |
| The grain of cast plaster against bone | the chamber |

You never pick anything up. You look at a thing, and if you have learned the
relevant tell, the HUD writes a year next to it in your list. The list is the
save file and the score.

**The distribution is the puzzle.** Nothing in this world is from one year.
Everything honest is smeared across 1946–1979. And then you notice that one
group of objects — the altar, the stakes, the ochre, the eye, the charms, the
tallies, the film — is all from **eight months of 1977**, and nothing is ever
that tidy unless somebody made it.

## 3 · Three layers

The game is the same site read three times.

### Layer one — it is a haunted mine (first 40 minutes)
You walk the parcel. The adit is genuinely frightening: it is dark, it is a
long way in, the chamber is exactly as advertised. If you stopped here you
would write "occult site, condition poor" on the form and leave.

### Layer two — it is a set (the middle two hours)
The tells start disagreeing with the story.

- **The snack bar** still has the unsold stock. Among the souvenir pamphlets is
  the **eleventh brochure** — same art, same OPEN DAILY, a price, and a map on
  the back with a numbered route: the nine rooms, the bar, the turn-off, the
  adit. *You have been walking somebody's tour.*
- **The nine dressed rooms** are the nine on the brochure map. They are not
  lived in. Everything personal in them is the same six brands. Every ashtray
  is at the same angle. There is no dust shadow behind any of it, because it
  was put down after the dust.
- **The Canteen** is where it was planned: the committee, at the back booth,
  on napkins. A sketch of the chamber, to scale, with arrows. A receipt book.
  A column of six names and what each was owed.
- **The filling station** has the hand-painted turn-off sign in the service
  bay, never hung, and the board of keys the tour used.
- **The trailer** is the workshop. The sign-painter lived in it. The same ochre
  pigment, in a tin, with the price on it. Plaster. Bags of it. Offcuts of the
  "bone." Cord on a reel, the same cord. And **a two-part mould for a skull.**
- **The drive-in** is the projection room, the flashlight, and reel 3 — which
  is not the lure film. It is what the brother-in-law shot while they were
  building it. Ninety seconds of six people laughing, aging rock with a wire
  brush, arguing about whether a hundred and twenty tallies is too many.

At the end of layer two the game has taken the fear away from you deliberately,
and you feel slightly cheated, and that is the setup.

### Layer three — one thing will not date (the last half hour)
Go back to the chamber with everything you now know.

Nine stakes. Three of them carry a skull. Two are plaster, out of the mould in
the trailer — you can see the seam, and the seam matches. **The third is not
plaster.**

And once you are looking properly:

- The film has a cut in it. The camera is running, somebody off-frame says
  something, and the next shot is a different day.
- The eye is painted on the **west wall** — the far one, the one the torch
  reaches last. Everything else in the chamber is arranged to be seen from the
  doorway. The eye is arranged to be *in front of something*.
- The wall behind it is not hewn like the other three. It is stacked. It is a
  seal, and the tells date it to **1911**, which is sixty-six years before
  anybody was making anything out here.
- The three wooden crosses in the wire fence out west — no names, never any
  names — date to 1911 as well.

The adit was a working silver mine and it came down on three men, and the
company wrote it off and moved on, and in 1977 six people picked it because it
was atmospheric and built a fake haunting against the wall the company had
stacked. One of them worked it out in August 1979. That is why the eleventh
brochure is still in a box in the snack bar and not in a rack. That is why
nothing was ever taken down. Nobody wanted to be the person who called it in,
so they all just stopped going.

## 4 · Why the endings are hard

No ritual, no order to enter, no reset. One decision, made with a clipboard.

- **Call it in.** The payphone at the kerb outside the Canteen is the only
  working line on the parcel. You report three unrecovered men in a
  non-permitted adit on land going to auction in nine days. What happens next
  is surveyors, a fence, and the end of the story. The sun comes up — properly,
  for the first time in the game — on a site that is now a job.
- **Put the eye back.** Nothing you do can reach them and everybody who owed
  them anything is dead. You re-seal it, and the legend, which is the only
  thing that ever brought a single human being down this road on purpose,
  stays intact and stays a lie. *You do not tell anyone, and the game does not
  make you feel good about it.*
- **Finish the inventory.** You have twenty-four rooms to count and this is
  not your parcel and not your century. You walk it, you write it down, the
  vacancy counter goes to 24/24, and you hand in the paper. The most ordinary
  option on offer and by far the worst thing you can do.

The game never says which is right. The three endings differ only in what the
last frame is: a fence, a wall, or a form.

## 5 · What each place is for

Everything in this list is already built.

| Place | Role |
|---|---|
| **Motel, nine lit rooms** | the walk-through. Wing A lights 102, 105, 107, 108, 203, 206; wing B lights 110, 112, 209 — nine of twenty-four, and the HUD has read 15/24 OPEN since long before there was a plot |
| **Office** | the paperwork, the key board, your own list |
| **Room 206** | the set they left running, because the tour needed one room to look *just* vacated |
| **The Rusty Canteen** | where it was planned. The back booth, the napkins, the receipt book, six names |
| **The stage** | the Salt Flats Revue, August 1979 — the last night anyone came, and the night one of them found out |
| **The snack bar** | unsold stock, and the box the eleventh brochure never left |
| **The drive-in + projection room** | the lure film ran here; reel 3 is the making-of |
| **The filling station** | the unhung turn-off sign, the key board, a meter you can read |
| **Somebody's trailer** | the workshop. Pigment, plaster, cord, and the mould |
| **The adit** | all three layers, in the same thirty metres |
| **The chamber's west wall** | 1911 |
| **Three graves, no names** | 1911 |
| **The burying ground** | 1890s–1960s. A real one, for contrast — this is what a genuine article looks like, and the game needs you to have seen one |
| **The payphone** | the only working line. Ending A happens here |
| **The phone booth alone in the desert** | a gimmick they installed and wired to nothing. It is on the brochure map. It does not ring, and that is the point |
| **The radio mast** | the county's. Its light is on a timer. **A deliberate red herring** — a world where every object is a clue is a puzzle box, not a place |
| **What is left of a motel** | what happens to the ones that do not make something up |
| **Windmill, the fence that stops mattering, the cold fire** | nothing. Leave them alone |

## 6 · What would have to be built

Lighter than `PLOT.md`, because there is no ordered-input puzzle and no
ritual state machine.

1. **An examine interaction** — `E` on an object, a close-up, and a line of
   text. Same requirement as the other outline.
2. **The dating list** — one screen. Object, place, year, and how you know.
   Sorted by year, which is what makes the 1977 cluster visible without the
   game ever pointing at it.
3. **Tells as unlocks** — a handful of flags. You cannot date a bottle until
   you have looked at the crate of empties. Cheap, and it is the whole
   progression system.
4. **The eleventh brochure** — one more `pic:brochure:` slot in the existing
   style, plus a map on the reverse. Half a day.
5. **Nine dressed rooms** — set dressing plus one "no dust shadow" tell each.
6. **The mould, and two seams** — the single most important object in the game
   is a plaster seam. It needs to be legible at examine range.
7. **The west wall** — a second state for that piece of the chamber, and the
   eye coming off it.
8. **Reel 3** — sixty to ninety seconds. Still the biggest art cost, but an
   easier one than the other outline's: six people in daylight with tools,
   not a ritual.
9. **Three endings**, which here are three final shots and a line of text.

---

## 7 · Where this is better, and where it is worse

**Better:** it reinterprets the occult chamber instead of taking it at face
value, so the most expensive thing already in the world gets used twice. It
needs no supernatural rules, so nothing has to be explained. The theme is
native to the road — a highway that ran on invented wonder, and the one place
that invented something and hit rock. And the red herrings are real herrings,
which makes the world feel like a place rather than a machine.

**Worse:** the middle hour is deliberately deflating, and a player who quits
during the "it was all fake" stretch leaves with the worst possible version of
the game. It also asks more of the writing than the other outline does — layer
two is entirely documents, and documents have to be *good* or it is homework.

---

## 8 · Open questions

- **Do the six get names?** I think yes, and I think you meet them in the film
  before you know what you are looking at, so the third act has faces in it.
- **Is the player's own name on the paperwork?** In `PLOT.md` I argued for
  leaving the tenth line blank. Here I would fill it in. The joke of this one
  is that you are an official.
- **How much does the game confirm?** My instinct is that it never says "1911"
  out loud — the list says it, because you dated it, and that is the only
  place it is ever written.
- **Does the third skull need to be seen?** Or is knowing enough? I would show
  it once, from the doorway, in the dark, and never again.
