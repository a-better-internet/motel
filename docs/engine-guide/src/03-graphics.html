Chapter two

Graphical style

The look in one sentence: painted, literal colour under soft desert light. Every surface is a hex someone chose, shaded by a warm sky, one hard sun and a handful of lamps, with the brightest highlights rolled off rather than clipped. It is closer to a diorama than to photorealism, and it holds together because nothing is allowed to go flat white or flat black.

The tone-mapping shoulder is the single most important line for the look. Painted colours stay exactly as painted, because that was the reason for avoiding filmic curves. But a white lounger in full sun leaves the shader at 1.8, and without the shoulder every face of it clips to the same 255,255,255 and loses its form. The shoulder works per channel, so a warm highlight stays warm instead of being pulled towards grey.

T.ShaderChunk.tonemapping_pars_fragment = T.ShaderChunk.tonemapping_pars_fragment.replace(
  "vec3 CustomToneMapping( vec3 color ) { return color; }",
  `vec3 CustomToneMapping( vec3 color ) {
     color *= toneMappingExposure;
     const float K = 0.76;                               // where the shoulder starts
     vec3 lo = min( color, vec3( K ) );
     vec3 hi = max( color - vec3( K ), vec3( 0.0 ) );
     return lo + ( 1.0 - K ) * ( vec3( 1.0 ) - exp( -hi / ( 1.0 - K ) ) );
   }`);
renderer.toneMapping = T.CustomToneMapping;

Six layers of light

Real-time lights are expensive and three.js recompiles shaders when their count changes, so the engine keeps the number of real lights fixed and builds everything else out of materials that glow.

#LayerWhat it isUsed for
1Base fillHemisphereLight sky #ffe7c4, ground #4a2a18, 0.72 by day; AmbientLight #fff0dc 0.15 to 0.21The sky and the warm ground bouncing light into every shadow, so nothing goes black
2The keyOne DirectionalLight: the sun by day (#ffeed0, 1.12), the moon by night (#8ea6d4, about 0.38)The only shadow caster in the world
3Lamp poolTwelve PointLights, no shadows, moved every frame onto the twelve registered lamps nearest the camera (within 46 m)Every fixture that has to actually light a room: sconces, porch lights, strip lights, desk lamps
4Glowing surfacesMeshStandardMaterial with emissive, registered in GLOW and ramped by the clockLampshades, ceiling fittings, vending fronts, lit windows, bulkheads
5Unlit facesMeshBasicMaterial with toneMapped:false; neon panels run 0.40 + 0.60 × darkNeon and lit signs, headlamp lenses, TV screens, fairy lights
6Faked lightAdditive sprites and transparent discsCandle pools on tables, the sun and moon discs, lamp halos, the pool's night glow

On top of those sits the flashlight (L): a SpotLight moved onto the camera every frame (intensity 2.45, 34 m throw, cone 0.40 rad, penumbra 0.55, no shadow) plus a weak 6 m point light half a metre ahead, so the floor at your feet is lit too.

Registering a lamp

LAMPS.push({ x, y, z,                   // world position of the bulb
  color: 0xffd8a0, intensity: 0.42,      // colour and strength before the clock multiplier
  dist: 8, decay: 1.5,                   // range in metres; 1.5 is gentler than physical, so no hot spots
  indoor: true,                          // burns at max(dark, 0.70) all day
  vol: [x0,x1, z0,z1, y0,y1],            // only on while the CAMERA is inside this box
  flicker: false, mothy: false });       // a bad ballast; insects circle it at night

Every frame updateLights() collects the lamps within 46 m whose volume contains the camera, sorts them by distance and hands the nearest twelve to the pool. The clock multiplier is dark for outdoor lamps, max(dark, 0.70) for indoor ones, and 1 for anything inside an underground volume.

Rules for light
  • An emissive does not illuminate. A glowing candle flame lights nothing. If a thing must light a room, register a lamp; if the twelve are spoken for, fake the pool of light with an additive disc on the table under it.
  • A lamp is a lens with a dark body. emitMat() gives a glowing surface a near-black diffuse colour, so the point light a few centimetres away cannot add its own light on top of the emissive and push it past white.
  • A lampshade is a tube, and a ceiling fitting is a bowl. A closed cylinder puts a flat glowing disc across the mouth of the shade, and that becomes the brightest flat area in the room. Use an open, double-sided tube with a bulb inside, and a half-dome under a ceiling rose.
  • A lamp with a volume is on only while you are inside it. That is what stops light leaking through walls. For a room you look into but never enter, such as the living room behind the observation glass, widen the volume to include where the watcher stands.
  • Glow goes through a soft knee. Emissive intensity is linear up to 0.55 of the brightest channel and then eases towards 0.92, so an amber lamp stays amber however hard it is driven.

Shadows that touch what casts them

Only the key light casts shadows. Its shadow camera is an orthographic box 92 m across that follows the player, snapped to whole shadow-map texels so the shadows do not crawl as you walk. The light sits 110 m out along the sun direction from that target, so the depth range is only ±100 m, which is what lets the bias be tiny.

key.shadow.mapSize.set(3072, 3072);            // 3 cm a texel across 92 m
const ksc = key.shadow.camera, KEY_D = 110;
ksc.left = -46; ksc.right = 46; ksc.top = 46; ksc.bottom = -46;
ksc.near = KEY_D - 100; ksc.far = KEY_D + 100;
ksc.updateProjectionMatrix();                  // without this, none of the above takes effect
key.shadow.bias = -0.00005; key.shadow.normalBias = 0.012;
const SHADOW_SNAP = (ksc.right - ksc.left) / key.shadow.mapSize.x * 2;

// every frame: follow the player in whole texels, then stand KEY_D out along the sun
const kx = Math.round(camera.position.x / SHADOW_SNAP) * SHADOW_SNAP;
const kz = Math.round(camera.position.z / SHADOW_SNAP) * SHADOW_SNAP;
key.position.set(kx + sunDir.x*KEY_D, kty + sunDir.y*KEY_D, kz + sunDir.z*KEY_D);
key.target.position.set(kx, kty, kz);
key.castShadow = dn.sunHeight > 0.04;          // the moon is too weak to shadow anything but acne
Rules for shadows
  • Tell the shadow camera. An OrthographicCamera builds its projection once. Change its bounds without calling updateProjectionMatrix() and the world is shadowed through the stock 10 m box. That is why the motel once cast no shadow on its own car park.
  • Flat things do not cast. When the merge bakes a bucket it splits off every piece thinner than 12 mm (decals, rugs, stains, the lot surface) into a second mesh that receives but does not cast. A plane in the depth map sits exactly on the surface it shades and makes acne.
  • See-through things do not cast. Transparent, additive and depth-less materials are excluded, or every window pane and light shaft prints a solid shadow.
  • Do not raise normalBias to cure acne. A big normal bias lifts the shadow off the foot of whatever casts it, so every post stands on a strip of daylight. Find what is shadowing itself instead. Measured result: the gap between a post and its shadow went from 11 cm to under 2 cm.

Under the ground there is no sky

Indoors there is still a sky outside the window, so the hemisphere light stays on. Underground there is not. addBuried(x0,x1, z0,z1, y0,y1, amb) registers a volume; while the camera is inside one, the loop fades over about a third of a second to:

  • sun intensity × 0 (taken away outright);
  • hemisphere intensity × 0.18, its colours pulled to lamplight (sky #ffd2a0, ground #3a2418);
  • ambient raised to 0.44 in the volume's own colour amb: warm #ffdcb4 for a lamplit bar, grey-green #d8dcd4 for a tiled corridor, near-dark #585c5c for an observation room.

Lamps inside a buried volume ignore the clock and burn at full strength, and so do emissive materials of the "buried" kind. Walking down a stair becomes a transition rather than a cut.

Atmosphere

Linear fog does the work of distance. By day its colour equals the sky's horizon colour, so a far mesa melts into heat haze. At night the haze is pulled towards #0a1020 and made darker than the sky, or the mountains wash out into it. Fog starts at 185 m and is full at 1,460 m by day (237 m and 1,790 m at night), and a dust gust tints it and pulls it in by up to 46%. The sky itself is a 2,400 m sphere drawn from a 16 × 256 canvas gradient that is repainted every frame (chapter 5). Three cloud layers (cumulus, altocumulus, cirrus) are sprites drawn on canvases, tinted by the sky each frame, and drifting downwind.

Textures: drawn, not loaded

Every map in the world is drawn into a <canvas> at load time, almost always in three passes: a base fill, thousands of tiny noise dots in two or three tints, then strokes and features (joints, grain, cracks, trowel swirls). Sizes run from 64 to 512 px. The result is flagged sRGB, set to repeat, and given anisotropic filtering.

TEX.stucco = (function(){                         // slate-periwinkle motel walls
  const c = cvs(256,256), x = c.getContext("2d");
  x.fillStyle = "#8e9cb4"; x.fillRect(0,0,256,256);                     // 1 · base fill
  noiseDots(x,256,256,2600,["rgba(255,255,255,0.06)","rgba(0,0,0,0.06)",
                            "rgba(120,140,170,0.10)"]);                 // 2 · noise
  for(let i=0;i<70;i++){                                               // 3 · trowel swirls
    x.strokeStyle = "rgba(255,255,255,"+(0.02+Math.random()*0.05)+")";
    x.lineWidth = 1+Math.random()*3; x.beginPath();
    x.arc(Math.random()*256, Math.random()*256, 6+Math.random()*22, Math.random()*6, Math.random()*6);
    x.stroke();
  }
  return rep(setSRGB(new T.CanvasTexture(c)), 1, 1);
})();

World-scaled UVs

boxGeo(w, h, d, uvScale) rescales each face's 0–1 UVs by that face's real size, so one texture tiles at the same density on a kerb and on a wall. uvScale is repeats per metre (0.35 by default; brick around 0.8; fine fabric 2 or more). This is what lets thousands of differently sized boxes share one material and merge into one mesh. planeGeo(w, h, uvScale) does the same for planes.

Colour comes from the vertex, and can only darken

Bucket materials are white, or carry a neutral map. Each piece pushed into a bucket carries its own hex, which the merge writes into a vertex-colour attribute. A vertex colour multiplies the map, so it can only ever darken the texture under it. Pick the bucket for the colour you want to end at, not for the material you would name the thing after. Twenty-four "spalled concrete" patches pushed into the gravel bucket came out orange-red, because the gravel map is #8a5f45.

Decals: a different crop every time

  • streakGeo(w,h) and cellGeo(w,h,cols,rows) pick a random cell of a 2×2 decal sheet, at a random offset, scale and flip. Each sheet fades to nothing at its margins, so a crop never lands on a hard edge and forty stains never repeat the same shape.
  • tagGeo(w,h,ci,ri) names its cell, for graffiti where the pieces are not interchangeable.
  • puddleGeo(rx,rz) is an irregular disc lying in xz. A rectangle of dark water reads as a slab someone laid on the floor.
  • runnerGeo(L,W) keeps a rug's border on its long edges and its fringe on its ends, which is how rugs are made.

Stains and age that must sit in a surface use a lit material with a vertex colour below the surface's own. An unlit stain is a constant brightness, so in the dim half of a room it ends up brighter than the plaster and reads as fog on the wall.

Signs and printed faces

const tex = signTex(Math.round(w*240), Math.round(h*240), (x, W, H) => {   // ~240 px a metre
  x.fillStyle = "#24365a"; x.fillRect(0, 0, W, H);
  x.fillStyle = "#e8e6dc"; fitText(x, "STAFF ONLY", W*0.88, H*0.62, W/2, H/2);
});
signPanel(w, h, tex, x, y, z, ry, lit);   // a plane with polygonOffset; lit = unlit basic material

fitText (Oswald) and fitSerif (Georgia) shrink the font until the words fit, and clamp every request so lettering never comes within 8% of the board's edge and is never taller than 62% of it. Sign faces use polygonOffset (factor −2, units −4) so they win the depth test against the board behind them at any distance. Lit faces are unlit basic materials; the neon panels registered in NEON run from 0.40 by day to 1.0 at night and stop there, because with tone mapping off anything above that clips and the lettering goes with it.

Image slots

Printed things such as brochures, postcards, framed prints, TV screens and vending fronts are slots with a fixed aspect ratio (brochure 4:9, postcard 2:3, wide print 2:1, TV 4:3, vending front 1:2). Each one draws canvas art as a fallback, and if a URL is set against its key in IMAGES the hosted picture replaces it at run time, provided the host allows cross-origin reads. GIFs are decoded by a small LZW decoder in 01-helpers.js, because browsers do not animate a GIF that is used as a WebGL texture. Procedural TV static runs underneath, so a set is never a frozen frame.

Painted hexes above about #c0 under full sun leave the shader past 1.0. The shoulder catches most of it, but keep sunlit paint at or below roughly #d8, give glowing materials a dark diffuse, and keep bright things such as ice, snow, porcelain and white paint a step below white. A bright surface keeps its edges only if it has room above it.

Buckets: how a world becomes a few hundred draw calls

Static geometry is never added to the scene directly. It is pushed into a named bucket; at the end of the build, bakeBuckets() merges each bucket into one BufferGeometry (positions, normals, UVs and vertex colours) with one material. The last smoke test counted 564 meshes for the whole world, including doors, cars and sprites.

// a bucket is a name and a material factory, declared once
bucketOf("plank", ()=>new T.MeshStandardMaterial({color:0xffffff, map:TEX.plank, roughness:0.72}));

// then anything, anywhere, pushes into it
push("plank", planeGeo(8, 8.4, 0.5), x, y, z, ry, "#9a6a44", -Math.PI/2, 0);
bx("block", w, h, d, x, y, z, 0.833, 0, "#d8d5ca");     // a box: centre x,y,z, then size
cyl("metal", rTop, rBot, h, seg, x, y, z, "#4a4e50");

// ...and at the very end, 15-bake.js:
const BAKED = bakeBuckets(scene);             // one mesh per bucket, plus a no-cast mesh for flats
buildColGrid();                                         // everything solid is in place by now

push(name, geo, x, y, z, ry, color, rx, rz) composes the matrix with Euler order YXZ, so a local tilt (rx, rz) is applied before the turn about the vertical (ry). y is always the centre of the piece. Things that move or change (door leaves, cars, the pool water, sprites, sign faces) are ordinary meshes outside the buckets.

The lens

three.js fixes the vertical field of view and lets the horizontal follow the window's shape. At the original 72° vertical a widescreen window was 104° across, and a flat projection stretches anything off-centre by 1/cos² of its angle, so a car visibly changed shape as you turned your head. The engine now aims for 88° across and clamps the vertical between 50° and 66°. The mouse wheel only offsets that (24° tighter, 6° wider).

function baseFov(aspect){
  const h = 88 * Math.PI/180;
  const v = 2*Math.atan(Math.tan(h/2) / Math.max(aspect, 0.2)) * 180/Math.PI;
  return Math.max(50, Math.min(66, v));
}

Modelling at this fidelity

Objects are built from boxes, cylinders, spheres, tori and a few extrusions. At this level of detail the silhouette is everything.

  1. Model the thing that has a silhouette. Four correctly proportioned club chairs read as "a pile of shapes". A wing chair, whose back is twice the height of its arms with wings coming forward, read at once. When a model will not read, choose a different object rather than refining the same one.
  2. Upholstery is not made of boxes. The parts you sit on are squashed spheres, arms are capsules, and only the hidden frame is boxes. An arm 25 cm above the cushion is an arm; 7 cm is a rail. The seat tucks under the arms rather than butting them.
  3. Chain anything made of tube. Ladders, rails and handrails are built so each piece starts where the last ended, with a quarter-torus at every bend. Built that way they cannot come apart.
  4. Know each primitive's axis. A cylinder is born along +y and a torus along +z, so the same rotation points them different ways. Work out the rotation for every piece; a back roll given rx instead of rz became a 74 cm log through a chair.
  5. A thing seen from a mile away gets one layer. Detail you cannot resolve averages into noise. A fire road seen from the valley is one strip, one colour, one width.
  6. Render it lit, close and from four sides before believing it. A walkthrough screenshot shows one angle, and one angle cannot tell a wrong shape from a wrong transform.
