Chapter three

Walking, running, sprinting, sitting

The player is not a physics body. It is a point with a radius, a pair of feet that follow the floor, and an eye 1.66 m above them. That simplicity is deliberate: it makes movement predictable, makes stairs and kerbs free, and means the player can never fall through the world or get stuck in it.

Heights in metres above the floor. The dashed box is the body that collision tests: radius 0.34, from 12 cm above the feet to 1.72 m. Anything lower than 12 cm, such as a kerb or a threshold, never blocks you.

The movement step

Each fixed step turns the keys into a direction relative to the view, moves on each axis separately so you slide along walls instead of sticking to them, and then lets the feet chase the floor.

const f = (keys.KeyW||keys.ArrowUp ?1:0) - (keys.KeyS||keys.ArrowDown ?1:0);
const r = (keys.KeyD||keys.ArrowRight?1:0) - (keys.KeyA||keys.ArrowLeft?1:0);
let sp = (keys.KeyQ ? SPRINT : (keys.ShiftLeft||keys.ShiftRight) ? RUN : WALK) * (underwater ? 0.45 : 1);
if(f || r){
  const len = Math.hypot(f, r), s = Math.sin(player.yaw), c = Math.cos(player.yaw);
  const dx = (-s*f + c*r)/len*sp*dt, dz = (-c*f - s*r)/len*sp*dt;   // forward is −z at yaw 0
  if(!blocked(player.pos.x+dx, player.pos.z, player.feetY)) player.pos.x += dx;   // slide on x…
  if(!blocked(player.pos.x, player.pos.z+dz, player.feetY)) player.pos.z += dz;   // …then on z
  player.step += sp*dt;
  player.bob = Math.sin(player.step*3.1) * 0.025;                   // ±2.5 cm, tied to distance
} else player.bob += (0 - player.bob)*Math.min(1, dt*6);
const target = surfaceY(player.pos.x, player.pos.z, player.feetY);
const rate = target > player.feetY ? 14 : 9;                        // step up snappily, drop softly
player.feetY += (target - player.feetY)*Math.min(1, dt*rate);

Eye level

The camera is placed once per frame from the player's state. The eye never has its own height; it is always a fixed distance above whatever the feet are on, which is why walking up a stair, onto a balcony or down a mine shaft needs no special code.

const eyeY = player.feetY + EYE + player.bob - player.sit*sitDrop;   // EYE = 1.66
camera.position.set(player.pos.x, eyeY, player.pos.z);
const cp = Math.cos(player.pitch);
camera.lookAt(player.pos.x - Math.sin(player.yaw)*cp,
              eyeY + Math.sin(player.pitch),
              player.pos.z - Math.cos(player.yaw)*cp);

Looking. Pointer lock on click, with drag-to-look as the fallback and touch drag on phones. Yaw changes by 0.0026 rad per pixel and pitch by 0.0024, with pitch clamped to ±1.15 rad (±66°). Keys are tracked by e.code in a keys map and all released on blur, contextmenu and when the tab hides, because the key-up for a key held while alt-tabbing never arrives.

What the feet stand on: surfaceY(x, z, currentY)

  1. Start from the ground. If the point is inside a void (a tunnel or room dug into a hill) at the current height, the ground is that void's floor. Otherwise it is groundY: the paved pad at 0, the pool basin, or Terrain.groundAt(x,z).
  2. Take the highest walkable surface you can reach. Walk the list of flats and ramps; any whose height is above the current best and no more than STEP_UP (0.62 m) above where you already are wins.

Because the test is against where you already are, standing on a hill above a tunnel gets you the hill, and walking into the tunnel's mouth gets you the tunnel. Bounds tests are strict, so two flats that only meet leave a line belonging to neither; grow every flat 10 cm into its neighbours, and make neighbouring voids overlap by 40 cm.

addFlat(x0, x1, z0, z1, y);                         // a level floor, deck, stair tread or threshold
addRamp(x0, x1, z0, z1, "z", a0, a1, y0, y1);       // y runs y0→y1 as z runs a0→a1
addVoid(x0, x1, z0, z1, y0, y1, floorY);            // in this box the ground is floorY, not terrain
Rule · You stand on the triangles that are drawn

Terrain is an analytic function drawn by sampling it on a grid and joining the samples with flat triangles. On a steep flank a 6 m cell can sit metres away from the function, so a player walking at the function's height sinks into the hill they can see. Terrain.groundAt finds the finest ring that actually draws a triangle at that point and interpolates within it. Use it for the player, the cars and anything placed on the ground.

Collision that never traps you

Colliders are axis-aligned boxes with a vertical span, {x0,x1, z0,z1, y0,y1}, so the same footprint can block the ground floor and leave the balcony above it open. blocked(x, z, feetY) grows each box by the player's radius and tests the body column from feetY + 0.12 to feetY + 1.72.

  • Static colliders go into a uniform 12 m grid once the world is built, padded by 1.4 m so a query near a cell edge still sees a box just over the line. Anything spanning more than 64 cells goes in a short list that is always checked.
  • Moving colliders (parked cars) live in COL_DYN, outside the grid. Highway traffic has its own list that only cars test, because a pedestrian held still inside a passing truck reads as being stuck, not hit.
  • Never stuck. If the player's own position is ever blocked (a door swung onto you, you stood up into a table), a ring search finds the nearest free spot within 6 m at a height you could have reached, and puts you there. It runs only while you are actually stuck.
function nearestFree(x, z, base, maxR, tol){         // rings 0.45 m out, 0.35 m apart
  for(let r = 0.45; r <= (maxR||4.2); r += 0.35){
    const n = Math.max(8, Math.round(r*14));
    for(let k = 0; k < n; k++){
      const a = (k/n)*Math.PI*2 + r;                   // each ring rotated a little
      const nx = x + Math.cos(a)*r, nz = z + Math.sin(a)*r;
      const fy = surfaceY(nx, nz, base + 0.45);
      if(Math.abs(fy - base) < (tol||1.3) && !blocked(nx, nz, fy)) return [nx, nz, fy];
    }
  }
  return null;
}

Sitting and lying down

A seat is a registration, not an object: a point on the cushion, a height, the direction the sitter faces, and a name for the prompt. Any piece of furniture becomes sittable with one line.

addSeat(x, z, y, yaw, label, lie);
// x, z   the middle of the cushion          y     the cushion's height (a chair ~0.50, a bed ~0.77)
// yaw    the CAMERA yaw when seated         label shown in the prompt: "C · SIT · THE OBSERVER'S CHAIR"
// lie    true for a bed or a couch you lie on

How it behaves

  1. Finding a seat. C takes the nearest seat within 2.0 m horizontally and 1.7 m vertically. Sitting has its own key because chairs and doors are often both in reach, and when they shared E the door won.
  2. Settling. Your yaw snaps to the seat's, and position and feet ease onto the cushion at rate 7 per second while the sit factor eases to 1 at rate 5. The eye ends at seat.y + 1.66 − sitDrop, with sitDrop 0.66 for sitting and 1.34 for lying. Lying also tips the view up to at least 0.86 rad, because on your back you look at the ceiling.
  3. Looking. While seated the mouse looks anywhere; movement keys do nothing until they have been released once. You almost always arrive at a chair still holding W, and without that guard you would stand up on the very next step.
  4. Standing up. Any movement key (or C again) stands you up. The engine tries spots in a fan in front of the seat, at 0.95, 1.35 and 0.65 m (1.45 to 2.25 m for a bed), sweeping ±0.52 rad steps either side of the way you faced. If every one is blocked it falls back to the ring search, so you can never be left standing inside the furniture.
Rule · A seat looks the other way from its chair

A chair is modelled facing its own +z and placed with ry, so it faces world (sin ry, cos ry). The camera looks down its own −z, along (−sin yaw, −cos yaw). So the seat that looks the way the chair faces has yaw = ry + π. Aim a chair at what it faces with ry = atan2(tx − cx, tz − cz), then add π for the seat. Getting this backwards shipped three chairs facing the wall.

Tuning note

With sitDrop at 0.66 the seated eye is 1.00 m above the cushion, which is about 1.5 m above the floor on a dining chair. A real seated eye is nearer 0.75–0.80 m above the cushion. If your world is about sitting, at a bar, in a car, in a theatre, raise sitDrop to about 0.9 for a lower, more natural seated view, and check that tables still clear the eye.
