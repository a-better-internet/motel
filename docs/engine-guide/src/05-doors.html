Chapter four

Doors and doorways

The world has forty-six working doors, and almost every one of them shipped blocked at least once: by a wall course nobody split, a lintel collider hanging at head height, a sign screwed to the leaf's centre line, a face left across an opened corridor, or a sofa between the door and the rest of the room. A door is two separate things: the leaf, which is easy, and the hole, which is where everything goes wrong.

The leaf

makeDoor(xf, lx, ly, lz, label, opensIn, ajar, style);
// xf       a local frame from XF(ox, oz, ry): the hinge is at local (lx, lz), the leaf extends +x from it
//          (ry = 0: leaf runs along world +x · ry = π/2: leaf runs along world −z)
// ly       the bottom of the leaf, 2 cm above the floor
// label    the prompt text ("E · STAFF ONLY"); a 3-digit label reads "ROOM 107"
// opensIn  which way it swings; ajar  how open it rests (0–1); style  which leaf

Each leaf is its own small merged mesh inside a Group whose origin is the hinge, so opening it is one rotation: g.rotation.y = base + swing × open, with swing = ±1.52 rad (87°). Every face detail (panels, kick plates, push bars, louvres) is built on both faces of the leaf, each layer at its own depth, so it never flickers.

Behaviour

  • Doors open themselves. On the same floor (feet within 1.8 m of the door's height) and within 2.5 m, a door's target is open; otherwise closed. Openness eases towards its target at rate 5.2 per second.
  • E overrides. Pressing E on the nearest door (within 3.4 m) forces it open or shut until you walk more than 4.6 m away, after which it goes back to automatic.
  • ajar is a minimum. A door with ajar = 0.34 never closes past a third open, like the room at the far end of the walkway with the light on and nobody in it.
  • The leaf has no collider. A door can never trap the player or hold them in a doorway, which means the opening itself must be kept clear of everything else.

The hole

Every doorway in the newer parts of the world is built from the same recipe. Here it is as the STAFF ONLY door through the rink's east wall: a 0.50 m painted block wall with a 2.14 m door head.

const AD = ARENA_DOOR, AD0 = AD.z - 0.54, AD1 = AD.z + 0.54;         // a 1.08 m opening
wall("block", RX1, RX1+RWT, LZ1,      AD0-0.06, -0.1,      RH+0.4);   // jamb wall, one side
wall("block", RX1, RX1+RWT, AD1+0.06, RZ1+RWT,  -0.1,      RH+0.4);   // jamb wall, the other
wall("block", RX1, RX1+RWT, AD0-0.06, AD1+0.06, AD.h+0.06, RH+0.4);   // the lintel, over the head
// frame posts and head trim, proud of both faces of the wall
for(const z of [AD0-0.03, AD1+0.03])
  m.B("metal", RWT+0.12, AD.h+0.08, 0.08, RX1+RWT/2, FY+(AD.h+0.08)/2, z, 0.4, 0, "#4a4e50");
m.B("metal", RWT+0.14, 0.08, AD1-AD0+0.16, RX1+RWT/2, FY+AD.h+0.04, AD.z, 0.4, 0, "#4a4e50");
// the threshold flat, overlapping both floors, and the floor finish through the opening
m.flat(RX1-0.2, RX1+RWT+0.2, AD0, AD1, FY);
m.P("lino", planeGeo(RWT, AD1-AD0, 0.6), RX1+RWT/2, FY+0.006, AD.z, 0, RUBBER, -Math.PI/2, 0);
// the leaf, hinged 6 cm inside the far post, and the sign on the wall above, not on the leaf
makeDoor(XF(W(RX1+RWT/2), Z(AD1-0.06), Math.PI/2), 0, GY+FY+0.02, 0,
         "STAFF ONLY", false, 0, "steel");
sign(0.86, 0.22, "STAFF ONLY", "#24365a", "#e8e6dc",
     RX1-0.012, AD.h+0.34, AD.z, -Math.PI/2);

The wall() helper draws a box and registers its collider in one call. A wall that starts on the floor gets a collider sunk half a metre below it, so there is no seam to slip under. A wall that starts higher up, which is a lintel, gets a collider that starts exactly at its own base:

const wall = (bk, x0,x1, z0,z1, y0,y1, c, uv) => {
  m.B(bk, x1-x0, y1-y0, z1-z0, (x0+x1)/2, FY+(y0+y1)/2, (z0+z1)/2, uv||0.833, 0, c);
  m.col(x0, x1, z0, z1, FY + y0 - (y0 <= 0 ? 0.5 : 0), FY + y1);     // a lintel stops where it is
};

Rule · Clear width is the structural opening minus what you line it with

A reveal that straddles its jamb stands into the opening on both sides. A 1.10 m structural opening with 4.5 cm of reveal each side leaves 1.01 m clear, which does not pass a 1.06 m leaf. That is a doorway blocked by arithmetic rather than by a wall.

Ten rules, each paid for

  1. A hole goes through every course, including the ones you cannot see. Skirting, wainscot, dado, cap rail, plaster lining, the tile skirt outside, the concrete mass under the floor: every layer that runs along a wall with a door in it must be split around the door. The diner's front door was blocked for rounds by three decorative courses that carried no collider at all.
  2. Nothing goes between the jambs but the leaf. Not a sign, not a hook, not a key board, not a "PRIVATE" card on the leaf's centre line. Signs go on the wall above the head, offset a centimetre along the wall's normal.
  3. A lintel stops where it is. Only colliders that start on the floor are sunk below it.
  4. An opened end gets no face. When a corridor that used to end in a wall is extended, the plane that closed it must go. A face has no collider behind it, so every collision probe walks straight through it while you stand looking at a grey wall.
  5. Probe a metre into each room, not just through the wall. A plaster lining stands clear of the wall on the room side, so a test the thickness of the wall misses it.
  6. Test the drawn world, not only the colliders. Geometry and collision disagree constantly. When a probe says a way is open and a screenshot says it is shut, the probe is testing the wrong thing.
  7. Getting to the door is part of the door. A doorway can pass every test and still be unusable because the route to it runs through a 20 cm slot between a desk and a soda machine. Flood-fill the whole floor from the building's front door every time a door goes into a furnished room.
  8. Lay out rooms from a plan with a named lane. Put a block of named anchors at the top of the room's code, including LANE, the strip from the door into the room that nothing may enter.
  9. A leaf never swings over a stairwell. If a door opens onto a flight, hang it to open the other way. The landing starts at the threshold.
  10. Put the lights where the door leads. A lamp's vol decides where it shines. A room seen through an open door is lit only if its lamps' volumes include the place you are standing.
