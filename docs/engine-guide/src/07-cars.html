Chapter six

Cars

Seventeen parked cars, every one of them drivable, and sixteen vehicles on the highway that react when you hit them. The car engine splits the problem the useful way: four springs own everything vertical (bounce, pitch, roll), and a two-axle tyre model owns everything flat (grip, slide, yaw). The two never argue because the tyre model never touches height.

How the cars look

A car is modelled in its own space (x right, y up, z forward, origin at the centre of mass) from about fifty primitives, each with a hex colour, then merged. Three body styles share one lower body and differ above the beltline.

The physics

Body and springs

The car is one rigid body of 1,200 kg with a box inertia (2.0 × 1.0 × 4.3 m; roll stiffened × 1.4) and a quaternion orientation. Each wheel is a spring that looks straight down: it asks surfaceY under its mount, the same function the player walks on, so cars drive up onto kerbs and forecourts rather than through them. If the ground is within rest length plus wheel radius, the spring pushes along the car's up vector at the wheel. Because the force is applied at the wheel and not at the centre, braking pitches the nose down and cornering rolls the body with no extra code.

const disp = CARC.restLength - (len - CARC.wheelRadius);    // how far the spring is compressed
const sv = up.dot(pointVelocityAtWheel);                     // how fast it is compressing
force.copy(up).multiplyScalar(CARC.springK*disp - CARC.damping*sv);
body.addForceAtPosition(force, wheelWorldPos);               // a torque about the centre of mass

Ride height. At rest each spring carries a quarter of the weight, so it sags m·g / 4k = 8.7 cm. CAR_RIDE = (rest − sag + radius) − mount = 0.96 m is where the body floats, and parked cars are built at exactly that height so nothing jumps when you get in.

Tyres: the bicycle model

With all four wheels down, the car is treated as two axles. Each axle's sideways force comes from its slip angle, clamped at a grip limit, and the difference between the front and rear forces, acting 1.56 m either side of the centre, turns the car.

steerAngle = steer * (1 - Math.min(speed, 40)/40) * 0.42;   // steering washes out with speed
const rot  = Math.atan2(yawRate, |vf|), slip = Math.atan2(vl, |vf|);
const latF = clamp(-5.0*(slip + rot - steerAngle), -2.6, 2.6) * axleLoad;   // axleLoad = m·g/2
let   latR = clamp(-5.2*(slip - rot),              -2.6, 2.6) * axleLoad;
if(handbrake) latR *= 0.5;                                  // the whole of "the back steps out"
let trac = 9000*throttle;  if(braking) trac = -12000*Math.sign(vf);
const drag = -5.0*vf*Math.abs(vf), roll = -15*vf;
yawAccel = (1.56*latF - 1.56*latR) / mass;

Airborne, collisions and the ground

  • In the air the tyres do nothing. Spin is split into yaw and everything else: pitch and roll bleed off fast (× e−3.6 dt), yaw persists (× e−0.45 dt), and a damped torque about up × worldUp rolls the roof back upright. W/S and A/D trim pitch and roll on the way down. Cars arc rather than tumble, and land driving.
  • Walls. Ten points round the shell (r 0.12) are tested against the static grid, the moving colliders and the traffic. Movement is reverted per axis, x then z, with a −0.18 bounce, so the car slides along a wall the way the player does.
  • Fast slopes. At 38 m/s a car covers two thirds of a metre a step, so a hillside can be above a wheel before its spring reacts. After every step the body is lifted out of the deepest wheel that has gone under, at up to 4 m/s.
  • No creep. With no throttle and under 0.5 m/s, planar velocity and yaw are zeroed, so a parked car never drifts.

The chase camera

const sp  = Math.min(1, Math.abs(speed)/28);                 // pulls back as you speed up
const horiz = (7.6 + sp*3.0) * Math.cos(camPitch);
target.set(car.x + Math.sin(a)*horiz,
           car.y + 2.20 + (3.4 + sp*1.3)*Math.sin(camPitch),
           car.z + Math.cos(a)*horiz);
target.y = Math.max(target.y, surfaceY(target.x, target.z, target.y) + 0.9);   // never under the ground
camera.position.lerp(target, 1 - Math.exp(-10*delta));       // exponential: same lag at any frame rate
look.lerp(car.position, 1 - Math.exp(-15*delta));
camera.lookAt(look.x, look.y + 0.85, look.z);
Rule · Draw the car between steps, and aim the camera at what is drawn

Physics runs at 60 Hz whatever the display does. savePrev() keeps exactly one step of history, and showCar() places the shell at lerp(prevPos, pos, stepAlpha) and slerp(prevQuat, quat, stepAlpha), with suspension travel and steer angle interpolated the same way. The camera then follows the shell, not the physics body; aimed at the body, the two were a fraction of a step apart every frame and the car shivered. Use 1 − e−kΔ rather than k·Δ for every follow, or the lag changes with the frame time.

Design note

A headlamp is a small bright thing that throws a large dim thing. The first version was one beam plus a pair of 3 m glow sprites, and it read as headlight-shaped clouds. Keep the lens the size of a lamp and put all of the size into the light it throws.

Parked cars and getting out

  • Parked cars are separate groups (not baked) with an axis-aligned collider in COL_DYN, sized from the rotated footprint: hx = |cos yaw|·1.03 + |sin yaw|·2.28.
  • Getting in copies the shell's transform into a fresh physics body and disables the car's own collider so it cannot block itself.
  • Getting out tries the driver's side, 1.9 m left of centre, with a tight height tolerance (0.45 m) before a loose one, so you never step out onto the shoulder a metre below where you got in. Then the collider is rebuilt round wherever the car stopped.

Highway traffic

Sixteen vehicles (sedans, pickups, and tractor-trailers) run on rails in two lanes 2.2 m either side of the centre line, at 26–32 m/s for cars and 19–25 m/s for trucks, and wrap at ±1,500 m, well past the fog, so nothing is ever seen appearing. Each keeps a moving collider that only cars test. At night their lamps are small bright lens planes with a faint additive halo behind.

Being hit moves the other car too. A struck vehicle gets a sideways velocity and a speed loss scaled by how hard you hit it, divided by its weight (trucks × 1.9). A damped spring (stiffness 6.4, damping 2.3) walks it back into its lane, overshooting once, and its heading is never animated; it is simply pointed along its own velocity with atan2(dir·speed, swayV). The slew, the correction and the fishtail all come from one number, so they cannot disagree with the path. Your car gets one impulse per collision, along the truck's direction and off the centre line, and is moved clear of the box if the space is free.
